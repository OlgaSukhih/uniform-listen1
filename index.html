Hi, I'm Thandi, and I'm sixteen. I go to a secondary school in Cape Town, South Africa. Our uniform is fairly traditional: a white shirt, a navy skirt or trousers, and a school jersey when it gets cold. We also have rules about shoes and jewellery. Not every school in South Africa has the same uniform, of course. It depends on the school. I quite like how you can recognise people from your school when you see them in town. It gives you a sense of belonging. But there's another side to it. School clothes can be expensive, especially if a family has several children. You may need specific items with the school badge, and you have to replace them as you grow. I don't think we need to get rid of uniforms completely. I'd just like schools to offer affordable options and be a little more flexible on very hot days. Looking smart is fine, but students also need to feel comfortable.

Read transcript

Hi, I'm Diego. I'm fifteen and I live in Mexico City. At my school, we actually have two uniforms. On ordinary school days, I wear a shirt, dark trousers and school shoes. There is also a jumper with the school logo. But on days when we have physical education, we wear a sports uniform with a tracksuit and trainers. I always look forward to PE days, and not just because I enjoy sport. The sports clothes are much more comfortable, especially when we have to move around a lot. Uniforms are common in Mexican schools, although the exact rules vary from one school to another. My parents like the idea because they say it makes mornings easier: I don't spend ages deciding what to wear. I can see their point. Still, I don't understand why our ordinary uniform has to be so formal. I'd be happy to keep a uniform if it were more practical. For example, a simple polo shirt and comfortable trousers would work well for most lessons.
